How to start exercising from zero

By the 3aash Ya Wa7sh team · General guidance for beginners, not a substitute for a doctor’s advice or a coach’s program.

Short answer: start with 15–20 minutes of brisk walking, three times a week, at a pace where you can talk but not sing. Add five minutes each week and two simple strength sessions at home. After four weeks you will be moving about two hours a week. If you have a health condition, check with your doctor first.

The first step is the hardest one, and you do not need a gym, expensive kit or a high fitness level to take it. What you need is a small plan you can actually keep, and to build on it slowly.

Before you start: safety first

If you have heart disease, high blood pressure or diabetes, feel chest pain or dizziness when you are active, are pregnant, take regular medication, or have an injury that still hurts — ask your doctor before starting any program. If you feel chest pain, severe breathlessness or dizziness while exercising, stop at once and get help.

How much activity do you need?

The World Health Organization recommends that adults aged 18–64 do 150 to 300 minutes a week of moderate activity such as brisk walking, or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous activity such as running, plus muscle-strengthening on two or more days a week (source: WHO). That is the goal — not the starting point. Any activity is better than none, and you will get there step by step.

Are you going at the right pace? The talk test

  • Light: you could sing — speed up a little.
  • Moderate (what you want): you can speak in full sentences but not sing.
  • Vigorous: a few words, then a breath — too early for this at the start.

Your first four weeks

A general plan for anyone starting from zero. If a week feels hard, repeat it before moving on — that is not failure, it is normal.

One strength round (no equipment)

  1. Sit-to-stand from a chair: 8–12 times, slowly.
  2. Wall push-ups: 8–12. When they get easy, move to a sturdy table.
  3. Glute bridge: 10–12.
  4. Plank on your knees: 15–30 seconds.

Rest a minute between rounds. The last two reps should feel a little hard — not impossible, and with no joint pain.

Tips that keep you going

  • Book a fixed time in your day like a meeting; early morning is often easiest, before the day fills up.
  • In the heat: train early in the morning or after sunset, drink water before and after, and wear light, pale clothes.
  • In Ramadan: an easy walk about two hours after iftar, or shortly before iftar if you feel well — and no hard efforts while fasting.
  • Log what you did: ticking off each session makes a real difference to sticking with it.
  • Find support: a friend to walk with, a group, or a small goal such as a 5K in a few months.

Common early mistakes

  • Starting with an hour every day, then getting exhausted or injured in week two.
  • Comparing yourself with someone who has trained for years.
  • Stopping completely after one missed day. Missed one? Just carry on tomorrow.
  • Relying on supplements. The basics are balanced food, good sleep and regular movement.

Common questions

How do I start exercising if I have never trained?

Start with 15–20 minutes of brisk walking three times a week, add five minutes each week, and add two simple home strength sessions. Consistency matters more than speed. If you have a health condition, ask your doctor first.

What is the best workout program for beginners?

The one you can stick to: moderate activity such as walking on most days, full-body strength work twice a week, and a small increase each week. At 3aash Ya Wa7sh, a professional coach writes a program around your time, place and fitness.

Can I start exercising if I am overweight?

Yes, and it is one of the best times to start. Begin with joint-friendly activities such as walking, swimming or a stationary bike, and build slowly. If you have high blood pressure, diabetes or knee pain, ask your doctor what suits you first.

How many times a week should a beginner exercise?

Three times a week is an excellent start, with a rest day in between. After a few weeks you can move to four or five active days, working towards the 150 minutes a week the World Health Organization recommends.

When will I notice results?

Many people find their breathing easier and stairs lighter within a few weeks of regular activity. Changes in weight and shape take longer and vary from person to person; food and sleep play a big part.
